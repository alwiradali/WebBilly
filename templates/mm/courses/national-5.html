Why N5 catches pupils out

National 5 is the first time Chemistry asks pupils to hold three different things at once: the chemical idea, the maths behind it, and the precise language the SQA wants back. Plenty of pupils understand the chemistry perfectly well and still lose marks because they wrote it the wrong way.

The other jump is calculation. Moles, concentration and mass calculations arrive quickly, and pupils who were comfortable in S3 can suddenly feel lost. It is almost never that they cannot do the maths — it is that nobody has slowed down and shown them how the calculation connects to what is physically happening.

That is what these classes are for. Small enough that gaps get spotted in the room rather than in the exam.

Is this the right class?

This class suits pupils sitting National 5 Chemistry who want to go into the exam knowing what to expect rather than hoping for the best. It works just as well for pupils who are coping fine and want an A as for pupils who have lost confidence and need the foundations rebuilt.

If your child is heading for Higher next year, N5 is the year that decides how hard that jump feels. Getting it solid now is far easier than repairing it later.
