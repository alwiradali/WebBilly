The National Curriculum

One curriculum. Four exam boards.

There is one National Science Curriculum in England & Wales, set by the Department for Education — and four different exam boards examining it. Two questions decide how your child should revise: which board they sit, and which course they are on.

Not sure? Ask Rod → See the four-week course
Question one

Which exam board is your child following?

AQA

Edexcel

Pearson

OCR

Eduqas

WJEC in Wales

The content is the same, however different exam boards organise the content in different ways. The exam papers are also written differently. Knowing which exam board your child is following is vital for exam preparation.

If you do not know, the school will tell you, and it is usually printed on the front of a past paper or a revision guide. Tell Rod when you enquire and the practice questions and past papers used in sessions will match it.

Question two

Which science course is your child following?

Guided learning hours are the teaching time each qualification is designed to take — the quickest way to see how much extra content the separate sciences carry.

Combined Science

240 guided learning hours

Awarded 2 Science GCSEs.

Biology

120 guided learning hours

All the biology content covered in Combined Science, plus approximately 40 additional guided learning hours. Awarded 1 Biology GCSE.

Chemistry

120 guided learning hours

All the chemistry content covered in Combined Science, plus approximately 40 additional guided learning hours. Awarded 1 Chemistry GCSE.

Physics

120 guided learning hours

All the physics content covered in Combined Science, plus approximately 40 additional guided learning hours. Awarded 1 Physics GCSE.

Combined or separate?

How SMARTin SCIENCE covers both routes

In most schools across the country, students follow the Combined Science route, with a smaller percentage on the separate science route. If your child is studying the latter, then we will cater for this.
