Before you enquire

The questions parents actually ask

Straight answers about how sessions work, what is covered, and how to start. If yours is not here, just ask — Rod answers his own messages.

Getting started

How do we get started?

Send an enquiry through the form on the home page or drop Rod an email. Tell him the year group, the exam board if you know it, and what is going wrong — whether that is one topic that never made sense or a general loss of confidence. Rod comes back with what he would suggest, what is available, and what it would cost. There is a free chat first, with no obligation, so you can see whether it feels like the right fit before anyone commits to anything.

Is it too late to start?

Usually not, but be honest with Rod about where things stand and he will be honest back. Starting in Year 10 gives the most room to fix things properly. Starting in the spring of Year 11 is tighter, and the work shifts towards exam technique and the topics carrying the most marks rather than covering everything. What is rarely true is that nothing can be done — most students are closer than they feel.

Do you offer a trial session?

Start with the free chat. It costs nothing, it tells Rod what is actually going on, and it tells you whether your child would get on with him. Fit matters more than people expect with tutoring: the same teaching lands very differently depending on whether a student feels comfortable admitting they are lost.

How sessions work

Are sessions online or in person?

Both. Rod is based in Leeds and teaches in person around Leeds and West Yorkshire, and online for students anywhere in the UK. Online is not a lesser option — past papers get shared on screen and annotated together, diagrams are drawn live, and students still have to explain things back. For a lot of families it is simply the version that gets done every week, because nobody has to drive anywhere.

What happens in a session?

Sessions follow a shape so nobody walks in wondering what is about to happen: what we are covering and why, the science explained in language the student actually uses, then questions that check whether it landed. Most sessions end with something to work on before the next one. Predictable structure is what makes hard science feel manageable.

One to one, or a group?

Both are possible and they suit different students. One to one moves at exactly the pace of the person in front of you. A small group can be better for a student who has gone quiet, because hearing someone else ask the question they were too embarrassed to ask is often the moment it clicks. Tell Rod which you think would suit and he will give you a straight opinion.

What do we need for an online session?

A device with a camera and microphone, a reliable connection, and somewhere reasonably quiet. A tablet works; a laptop is easier. It also helps to have paper and a pen to hand — writing science down is part of learning it, and it is much easier to spot a misunderstanding when you can see how a student has set out their working.

The science itself

Which exam boards do you cover?

The core of GCSE Combined Science is shared across AQA, Edexcel and OCR, and that is what the teaching is built on. What genuinely differs is the wording of questions, the layout of the paper and the shape of the mark scheme. Tell Rod which board your child's school uses and the practice work is matched to it — that detail is worth more marks than most people realise.

Do you cover Triple Science as well as Combined?

Ask. The brief here is GCSE Combined Science for Years 10 and 11, which is what most students sit. Triple shares a great deal of that content with extra topics on top, so it is often workable — but Rod will tell you honestly whether he is the right person for what your child needs rather than take it on regardless.

My child is fine at science but keeps losing marks. Can you help with that?

That is one of the most common reasons families get in touch, and it is very fixable. Marks usually go missing in the same handful of places: a “describe” question answered as if it said “explain”, a unit left off, a graph described but never interpreted, or a six-mark answer containing all the right science in no usable order. None of that is a knowledge problem, and none of it takes years to correct.

What about the required practicals?

They come up in the written papers, so they get taught properly rather than skimmed. That means the method, why each step is done that way, what the variables are, and the sort of question examiners actually ask about them. Students who can talk through a practical rather than recite it tend to pick up marks that others leave behind.

Between sessions

Is there homework?

Usually something small and specific — a set of questions, a past-paper section, a topic to bring back and explain. It is not busywork; the point is that a lot of the progress happens between sessions rather than during them. If a week is genuinely too full, say so, and Rod will pitch it accordingly rather than set something that will not get done.

How will I know if it is working?

You will hear it before you see it on a report. The early sign is a student explaining something at the kitchen table that they could not explain a month ago. Rod will tell you plainly how sessions are going, what has improved and what is still shaky — you should never have to guess whether your money is doing anything.

Workshops and clubs

What are the workshops and clubs?

Alongside tuition, Rod runs hands-on STEM workshops, after-school science clubs and holiday clubs. Workshops are one-off practical sessions for a class or group. Clubs run weekly through term time. Holiday clubs use longer blocks for the messier experiments that never fit a normal lesson. They are about curiosity as much as curriculum — details are on the workshops page.

Can our school or group book a session?

Yes — schools, PTAs, scout and guide groups, home-education networks and community groups are all welcome to ask. It helps to say how many students, what age, roughly where you are and what you are hoping for. Rod comes back on availability, what the session would look like and what it would cost for your group.

Practical questions

How much does it cost?

It depends on what you need — one to one, a small group, a workshop or a club — so rather than publish a number that turns out not to apply, Rod gives you a straight price for your situation when you enquire. Ask and you will get a clear answer with no obligation and no sales routine.

Is Rod DBS checked?

Rod is an experienced teacher and a former Head of Science who has worked in UK schools for over twenty years. Ask him directly about his current DBS status and safeguarding arrangements when you get in touch — it is a completely reasonable question and any good tutor will expect it.

What if we need to cancel or rearrange?

Life happens, especially with teenagers. Let Rod know as early as you reasonably can and he will do his best to move things. Ask about the current arrangements for cancellations when you book so everyone knows where they stand from the start.

Do you teach anywhere outside Leeds?

In person, Rod covers Leeds and West Yorkshire — there is a list of areas with a bit of local detail on each. Online, he teaches students anywhere in the UK, and that is genuinely the same teaching rather than a watered-down version. If you are further afield and not sure, just ask.
