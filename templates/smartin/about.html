From the front of the classroom

Rod Martin has spent over twenty years teaching science in UK schools, including running an entire science department as Head of Science. In that time he has taught every part of the GCSE Combined Science specification, more than once, to every kind of student — the ones who love it, the ones who dread it, and the very large group in between who simply lost the thread somewhere in Year 9.

That last group is the reason SMARTin SCIENCE exists.

Almost nobody is bad at science. They just met one topic that moved too fast, and everything built on top of it has felt shaky ever since.

Why four weeks

Long tutoring arrangements can drift. A fixed four-week course does not. Each week has one job — Biology, Chemistry, Physics, then exam technique — and every session ends with the student able to name something they can now do that they couldn't do an hour earlier.

Four weeks is also long enough to change habits: how to read a command word, how to structure a six-mark answer, how to rearrange an equation without panicking. Those habits carry into every paper afterwards, which is why the effect outlasts the course.

Knowing where the marks actually go

Having taught the specification and led a department, Rod knows exactly where students lose marks — and it is rarely where parents assume. It's the "describe" answered as "explain". The unit left off. The graph described but never interpreted. The six-marker that contains all the right science in no usable order.

These are fixable in weeks, not years. That's what week four is for.

The three values behind every session

  • Empowerment — students leave able to do the thing themselves, not just watch it be done.
  • Engagement — if a session is boring, it isn't working. Activities, comparisons and real examples do the heavy lifting.
  • Excellence — high expectations, clearly explained, with the support to actually meet them.
