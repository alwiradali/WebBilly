Revision

A four-week GCSE Science revision plan that actually gets done

By Rod Martin6 min readGCSE Combined Science · Y10 & Y11

Most revision plans fail in the same way. They are built in one optimistic evening, they assume four hours a night, and they collapse by Wednesday. Then the student concludes they are bad at revising, which is both untrue and expensive.

A plan that gets done is short, specific and repeatable. Here is one.

First: stop re-reading

Reading notes feels productive because the material looks familiar. Familiarity is not recall. In the exam nobody asks you to recognise the mitochondria — they ask you to produce it from nothing.

What works instead

  • Retrieval practice — close the book, write what you remember, then check.
  • Spacing — revisit a topic after a gap, not the same evening.
  • Interleaving — mix Biology, Chemistry and Physics rather than blocking one for a week.
  • Past questions — the closest possible practice to the real thing.

The daily unit: 25 / 5 / 10

One revision block is 25 minutes of focused work, a 5-minute break, then 10 minutes of retrieval on a topic from earlier in the week. Two blocks on a school night is plenty. Three or four at weekends.

Forty minutes done properly beats three distracted hours, and — more to the point — it still happens on the day someone has football practice.

The four-week plan

WeekFocusEach session
Week 1BiologyCells, organisation, infection, bioenergetics. End each block with 10 minutes of blank-page recall.
Week 2ChemistryAtomic structure, bonding, quantitative chemistry, chemical changes. Balance five equations daily.
Week 3PhysicsEnergy, forces, waves, electricity. Write the recall equations from memory every single day.
Week 4Exam techniqueTimed past papers. One full paper, then mark it against the scheme and list every lost mark.

Note what week four is not: more content. By then the content is in. Week four converts it into marks, which is a different skill entirely.

The mark-loss list

This is the highest-value ten minutes of the whole four weeks. After marking a past paper, write down every mark lost and why — not what the answer was. Categories usually fall out fast: “didn't know it”, “knew it, wrote it badly”, “misread the command word”, “arithmetic”, “ran out of time”.

Only the first category needs more revision. The other four need technique, and technique improves far faster than knowledge does.

Most students revise the topic they just got wrong. The better move is to fix the reason they got it wrong.

For parents

  • Ask “what did you do?” rather than “how long did you do?”. Time is a bad proxy for progress.
  • Offer to be tested at. Being the person holding the answer sheet is genuinely useful and costs nothing.
  • Protect one evening a week with no revision at all. Plans without rest days don't survive contact with reality.
  • Watch for the student who is always revising and never testing. That is anxiety, not preparation.

Print this bit

  1. Two blocks a night, three or four at weekends.
  2. Every block ends with ten minutes of blank-page recall.
  3. One full past paper a week from week two onward.
  4. Keep a running mark-loss list and read it before the next paper.
