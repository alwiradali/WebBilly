Inside Every Lesson

The same four phases, every single week

Predictable structure is what makes difficult science concepts feel manageable. Students always know what is coming next.

1
Connect

Connection to prior knowledge, and hooks students’ attention. Short tasks, provocative questions or real-world examples connect existing understanding to the lesson topic. It encourages thinking, recalling and predicting, setting the stage for focused learning.

2
Activate

Students engage actively with new content through hands-on activities, problem-solving and guided exploration. This phase emphasises inquiry-based learning, letting students wrestle with core concepts and discover understanding rather than passively receive it. It encourages curiosity and deeper cognitive engagement.

3
Demonstrate

Applying their learning to demonstrate understanding. Activities may include discussion, collaborative problem-solving, or creating something that reflects mastery of the lesson objectives — guiding students while promoting independence and self-monitoring.

4
Consolidate

Reinforces learning and assesses understanding. Students reflect on what they have learned, complete exit activities and discuss, so that knowledge is consolidated. This phase ensures the success criteria are met and provides formative assessment to inform what comes next.

  • Home learning tasks set at this stage, marked and returned with feedback, which also reaches parents
  • Further consolidation tasks — short video clips to watch between sessions to reinforce key concepts
  • Revision links and notes provided

Bacteria and viruses at a glance

This is what "making complex concepts simple" looks like in practice — one of the most commonly muddled comparisons on the Combined Science paper, sorted in a single table.

The Difference
Bacteria
Viruses
Size
Around 1,000 nm — visible under a light microscope.
Around 20–300 nm — only visible under an electron microscope.
Structure
Living cells with a cell wall, membrane, cytoplasm and loose DNA. No nucleus.
Not cells at all: genetic material inside a protein coat. No cytoplasm, no membrane.
Reproduction
Divide by binary fission — as often as every 20 minutes in ideal conditions.
Cannot reproduce alone. They invade a host cell and make it produce copies.
How they make you ill
By producing toxins that damage cells and tissues.
By bursting the host cells they have taken over.
Treatment
Antibiotics kill bacteria without harming body cells.
Antibiotics do nothing. Antivirals are difficult — the virus lives inside your own cells.
Exam examples
Salmonella · Gonorrhoea
Measles · HIV · Tobacco mosaic virus

Questions

Everything parents usually ask

Who is the tuition for?

Y9, Y10 and Y11 students sitting GCSE Science. It suits students who are capable but have lost confidence, students who have fallen behind after missing topics, and students who understand the content but keep losing marks in exams.

How long does it take to see a difference?

Sooner than most parents expect, because the first thing that shifts is not knowledge but approach — reading the command word, showing the working, structuring a longer answer. Those habits start paying back within a few sessions and they carry into every paper afterwards.

How long the whole thing runs depends on the student. Some want a short, focused block before an exam; others keep a weekly session going through Year 10 and 11. Ask and Rod will tell you honestly what he thinks yours needs.

Which exam boards do you cover?

The core content of GCSE Combined Science is shared across AQA, Edexcel and OCR, and that is what the course teaches. Tell me your child's board when you enquire and the practice questions and past papers used will match it.

Does it cover Biology, Chemistry and Physics?

Yes — all three, plus the maths and practical skills that run through every paper. That's the point of Combined Science: no student gets to avoid the one they find hardest.

What happens in a typical session?

The big idea at a glance, the key comparisons, a class worksheet, a hands-on activity, a short video to watch afterwards and set homework. The same structure every week, so students always know what to expect.

Will I hear how my child is getting on?

Yes. Homework is marked and returned, and progress feedback comes to you — not just to your child. You'll know what improved and what still needs work.

My child says they "just aren't a science person". Can you help?

That sentence is nearly always a confidence problem rather than an ability problem, and it usually starts with one topic that went past too quickly. Finding that gap and closing it is exactly what the first sessions are for.
