Week by week

What happens in each of the four weeks

Each week is self-contained but builds on the last. By week four, the science is familiar and the exam is just a format.

Week 01 · Biology

Biology — that actually sticks!

The topics students most often confuse, organised into clear, memorable comparisons.

  • Cell biology, transport in and out of cells, microscope calculations
  • Infection and response — bacteria, viruses, fungi and protists
  • Organisation: enzymes, digestion, the heart, lungs and blood
  • Homeostasis, inheritance, variation and ecosystems
  • The required practicals, and how they get examined
Week 02 · Chemistry

Chemistry — without the dread!

Marks often slip away because students do not fully understand the periodic table’s key knowledge and rules.

  • Atomic structure, the periodic table and the three types of bonding
  • Quantitative chemistry: moles, relative formula mass, percentage yield
  • Chemical changes, reactivity, electrolysis and energy changes
  • Rates of reaction, reversible reactions and organic basics
  • Balancing equations until it stops needing thought
Week 03 · Physics

Physics, formulas and maths skills — without the switch off!

Equations, graphs and scientific language. Students lose marks when they cannot choose the correct equation, identify its subject or rearrange it accurately.

  • Energy stores, transfers, efficiency and power
  • Forces, motion, distance–time and velocity–time graphs
  • Waves, the electromagnetic spectrum, electricity and magnetism
  • Rearranging any equation, and never losing a unit again
  • Reading data, plotting graphs, describing patterns
Week 04 · Exam technique

Turning knowledge into marks

The session that improves grades fastest, as many marks are lost through insufficient exam practice.

  • Command words: describe, explain, evaluate, compare, suggest
  • A structure for 6-mark questions that scores every time
  • Practical and data questions, done under timed conditions
  • Full past-paper practice with live, worked feedback
  • Rearranging equations following a system that ensures marks are not lost
  • A personal revision plan for the run-up to the real exams
Inside every lesson

The same four phases, every single week

Predictable structure is what makes difficult science concepts feel manageable. Students always know what is coming next.

1
Connect

Connection to prior knowledge, and hooks students’ attention. Short tasks, provocative questions or real-world examples connect existing understanding to the lesson topic. It encourages thinking, recalling and predicting, setting the stage for focused learning.

2
Activate

Students engage actively with new content through hands-on activities, problem-solving and guided exploration. This phase emphasises inquiry-based learning, letting students wrestle with core concepts and discover understanding rather than passively receive it.

3
Demonstrate

Applying their learning to demonstrate understanding — discussion, collaborative problem-solving, or creating something that reflects mastery of the lesson objectives, while promoting independence and self-monitoring.

4
Consolidate

Reinforces learning and assesses understanding. Students reflect, complete exit activities and discuss, so knowledge is consolidated and the success criteria are met.

  • Home learning tasks set at this stage, marked and returned with feedback, which also reaches parents
  • Short video clips to watch between sessions to reinforce key concepts
  • Revision links and notes provided
