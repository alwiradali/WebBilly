Week by week

What happens in each of the four weeks

Each week is self-contained but builds on the last. By week four, the science is familiar and the exam is just a format.

Week 01 · Biology

Biology that actually sticks

The topics that get muddled most often, sorted into comparisons students can actually hold in their heads.

  • Cell biology, transport in and out of cells, microscope calculations
  • Infection and response — bacteria, viruses, fungi and protists
  • Organisation: enzymes, digestion, the heart, lungs and blood
  • Homeostasis, inheritance, variation and ecosystems
  • The required practicals, and how they get examined
Week 02 · Chemistry

Chemistry without the fear

Where marks quietly disappear. Mostly because of equations and moles — both of which are habits, not talents.

  • Atomic structure, the periodic table and the three types of bonding
  • Quantitative chemistry: moles, relative formula mass, percentage yield
  • Chemical changes, reactivity, electrolysis and energy changes
  • Rates of reaction, reversible reactions and organic basics
  • Balancing equations until it stops needing thought
Week 03 · Physics

Physics, formulas and maths skills

Equations, graphs and the language. Physics punishes students who can't rearrange — so we drill it.

  • Energy stores, transfers, efficiency and power
  • Forces, motion, distance–time and velocity–time graphs
  • Waves, the electromagnetic spectrum, electricity and magnetism
  • Rearranging any equation, and never losing a unit again
  • Reading data, plotting graphs, describing patterns
Week 04 · Exam technique

Turning knowledge into marks

The week that changes grades fastest, because most lost marks aren't lost through ignorance.

  • Command words: describe, explain, evaluate, compare, suggest
  • A structure for 6-mark questions that scores every time
  • Practical and data questions, done under timed conditions
  • Full past-paper practice with live, worked feedback
  • A personal revision plan for the run-up to the real exams
