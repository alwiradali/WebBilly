Your money & your rent

How your rent is
protected.

Rent passing through a letting agent is only as safe as the agent holding it. Ours is protected by law, by scheme membership and by process — here is each layer.

Client Money Protection

Every penny of rent we hold on your behalf sits in a designated client account, separate from company money, and is covered by the Propertymark Client Money Protection scheme. If money held on your behalf were ever misappropriated, the scheme compensates you — this is a legal requirement for letting agents in England, and we hold it.

Rent collected & paid on time

Rent is collected on the due date, reconciled against your tenancy, and paid to you promptly with a statement you can actually read. Arrears are chased the moment they appear — not weeks later — and you are told immediately rather than discovering it on a statement.

Deposits held in the DPS

Tenant deposits are registered with the Deposit Protection Service within the statutory 30 days and the prescribed information is served correctly. That protects your position at the end of the tenancy: a deposit that is protected late, or not at all, can cost you a court penalty of one to three times the deposit — and every renewal counts as a fresh breach, so the figure compounds.

Your asset

How your property
is protected.

A tenancy is four things: the right tenant, the right paperwork, the right condition and the right eyes on it. We handle all four.

Inventory & condition

A dated, photographic inventory and schedule of condition at move-in, and a check-out report at the end. Without that evidence a deposit deduction is very hard to win at adjudication — with it, your claim stands up.

Routine inspections

Periodic inspections with written findings and photographs, so small problems — a slow leak, early damp, an unreported repair — are caught while they are still cheap to fix.

Compliance kept current

Gas Safety, EICR, EPC, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, and the Homes (Fitness for Human Habitation) standard — tracked with renewal dates so nothing lapses. Under the Renters' Rights Act and Awaab's Law the timescales for damp, mould and hazards are tightening; we work to them.

HMO & selective licensing

If your property is an HMO or sits in a selective licensing area, operating without the right licence risks a rent repayment order and an unlimited fine. We identify what is needed, apply, renew, and co-ordinate any works required to meet the standard.

Maintenance by trusted trades

Ten years of hands-on maintenance experience and a contractor list we actually use. Repairs are co-ordinated, quoted where they matter, and recorded — protecting both the tenant and your asset.

Correct paperwork

Tenancy agreements drafted properly, How to Rent guide served, EPC and gas certificate issued, deposit prescribed information served on time. Getting this wrong is what invalidates possession notices — so we treat it as the foundation, not the afterthought.

Tenant selection

How we find — and check —
good tenants.

The single biggest risk to a landlord's income is the wrong tenant. Referencing is not a formality here; it is the week of diligence that saves a year of trouble.

Marketed properly, to the right audience

Every adult occupier's immigration status is checked and documented before a tenancy begins, as required by the Immigration Act. Getting this wrong exposes the landlord to a civil penalty, so it is never skipped or assumed.

Credit history, employment and income verification, affordability assessment against the rent, and a previous landlord reference. We are looking for a tenant who can comfortably afford the property — not one who can only just reach it.

Identity verified against documents, with anti-money-laundering checks applied where they are required of a regulated agent.

The law, as it stands now

The Renters’ Rights Act,
in plain English.

This is the biggest change to private renting in thirty years, and most of it is already in force. Here is what actually changed for you as a landlord — and what we do about it.

No more fixed-term ASTs

Assured shorthold tenancies have been replaced by assured periodic tenancies. Every tenancy now rolls month to month with no fixed end date, and a tenant can leave on two months’ notice. In practice that puts a premium on choosing the right tenant at the start — which is the part we were always careful about.

Section 21 is gone

Since 1 May 2026 there is no such thing as a ‘no-fault’ notice. Possession now runs through Section 8 only, and you must have a stated legal ground — serious rent arrears, sale of the property, a landlord or family member moving in, or breach of the tenancy — with evidence to support it.

Grounds you can still rely on

You have not lost control of your property. If you need to sell it, move into it, or the rent is seriously in arrears, there are defined grounds for possession — each with its own notice period and evidence requirement. We keep the paperwork in the shape those grounds need from day one.

Rent rises follow one route

Rent increases now happen once a year through the statutory notice procedure, and a tenant can refer a rise they think is above market rate to the First-tier Tribunal. We price reviews from real local comparables, so the figure we put forward is one that stands up if it is ever tested.

Awaab’s Law and hazards

Damp, mould and other serious hazards now carry fixed timescales for investigation and repair. This is exactly why we inspect, photograph and log — a reported issue with a dated record and a contractor booked is a very different position from one discovered late.

What this means for you

Honestly? For a landlord who references properly, protects the deposit on time, keeps certificates current and fixes things when they break, very little has changed. The Act is hardest on landlords who were cutting corners. If you would like your current tenancies reviewed against the new rules, we will do it and tell you what needs tidying.

Regulation & client money

ARLA Propertymark

Propertymark is the professional body for the property sector; ARLA is its lettings arm. A Propertymark Protected agent must meet entry requirements, hold Client Money Protection and professional indemnity insurance, keep client money in a separate designated account, submit to an annual accountant's report, and follow the Propertymark Conduct and Membership Rules.

Client Money Protection is the part that matters most, and it is worth being precise about it. It is a reimbursement scheme: if an agent went under, or money held on your behalf were misappropriated, you claim from the scheme and are compensated. It covers rent, deposits held by the agent, repair floats and service charge funds. It has been a legal requirement for every letting agent in England since 2019 — but the client account behind it is only inspected annually by an independent accountant if the agent belongs to a body like Propertymark. We do.

So in practice: your money is ring-fenced in a designated client account, it is insured, the account is independently checked every year, and we answer to a professional body rather than only to ourselves.

Independent redress · membership T06217

The Property Ombudsman

Every letting agent in England must belong to a government-approved redress scheme. The Property Ombudsman provides free, independent adjudication for landlords and tenants when a complaint cannot be resolved with the agent directly.

If you were ever unhappy with how we handled something, you would not be arguing with us alone: you can escalate to an independent Ombudsman whose award is binding on us, up to the scheme's compensation limits.

Deposit protection

Deposit Protection Service

Every deposit taken on an assured tenancy must be protected in a government-approved scheme within 30 days of receipt, and the prescribed information — the scheme, the dispute process, the landlord’s details and how the money can be reclaimed — must be served on the tenant inside the same 30 days. We use the DPS, and we do both.

This protects you at least as much as the tenant. Under section 214 of the Housing Act 2004 a tenant can ask the county court to order a penalty of between one and three times the deposit if either duty is missed, and each renewal is treated as a separate tenancy — so a deposit that was never protected can generate that penalty several times over. At the end of the tenancy, disputes go to free adjudication decided purely on the evidence submitted. That is precisely why we photograph a proper inventory on the day the tenant moves in.

Legislation & best practice

NRLA

The National Residential Landlords Association is the UK's largest membership organisation for private landlords, providing training, legal guidance and up-to-date interpretation of housing law.

Membership keeps us current on the things that change fastest — the Renters' Rights Act, Awaab's Law on damp and mould, EPC requirements and licensing — so your property stays compliant as the rules move.

Landlord questions

Answers, plainly.

How is my money protected if something happened to the agent?

Rent and any funds we hold on your behalf are kept in a designated client account, separate from our own money, and are covered by Propertymark Client Money Protection. That scheme exists to compensate landlords and tenants where client money is misappropriated. We also hold professional indemnity insurance, and we are members of The Property Ombudsman for independent redress.

What checks do you run on a tenant before they move in?

Right to Rent immigration checks on every adult occupier, credit history, employment and income verification, an affordability assessment against the rent, a previous landlord reference, and identity verification. Where references are thin — students, for example — we reference a UK-based guarantor as well.

Who holds the deposit?

The deposit is registered with the Deposit Protection Service within 30 days of receipt and the prescribed information is served on the tenant. If there is a dispute at the end of the tenancy it goes to the scheme's free adjudication service, decided on the evidence from the inventory and check-out report.

What happens if the tenant stops paying?

Arrears are identified the day the rent is missed, not at month end. We contact the tenant immediately, keep you informed in writing, and follow a documented arrears process. If matters escalate we advise on the correct notice and, where you want it, work with solicitors on possession — with the paperwork in the state it needs to be in for a notice to be valid.

Which safety certificates does my property need?

Typically an annual Gas Safety Certificate, an Electrical Installation Condition Report at least every five years, a valid EPC at the required minimum rating, and compliant smoke and carbon monoxide alarms. HMOs carry additional fire safety and amenity requirements. We track renewal dates and arrange the inspections.

Can you take over a tenancy from my current agent?

Yes, and we handle the whole handover. You sign one letter of authority; we collect the keys, tenancy agreements, deposit registrations, certificates and tenant details from your existing agent, and write to your tenants explaining where the rent goes now. The tenancy itself is unaffected.

Do you manage HMOs and student properties?

Yes — HMOs and single lets are our core business, for both professionals and students, across Salford and Greater Manchester. That includes licence applications and renewals, selective licensing, preparing a property to the required standard, and HMO conversion work.

How quickly will I hear back about a professional valuation?

Usually within one working day. The professional valuation is free, carries no obligation, and is based on comparable lettings we have actually agreed nearby — an achievable rent rather than a flattering one that leaves your property empty.
