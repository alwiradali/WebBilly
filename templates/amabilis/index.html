How a cake happens

01

The brief

It starts with a message and a photograph you've been saving. Colour scheme, theme, the person it's for, the date it has to exist by.

02

The build

Sponges baked close to the day, cooled, levelled, layered, filled and chilled — then coated and sharpened until the edges are true. Most of the structure you never see.

03

The piping

Shells, ruffles and swags worked one at a time, straight from the bag. This is where the hours go, and where the price comes from.

04

The finish

Pearls, gold, edible print, fresh flowers cut and wired the same morning, and the lettering piped last of all.

05

The handover

Boxed, ribboned and photographed, then handed over on the doorstep at the time you agreed.

The baker

There is one person behind
Bakedwith Amabilis.

She takes the order and prices it. She buys the flowers, bakes the sponges, colours the buttercream, pipes every shell, letters the plaque, boxes it, ties the ribbon and hands it to you herself.

That's why cakes need a week and brownies need five days. It isn't a policy so much as arithmetic.

It's also why no two are the same. A cake made from your photograph will be its own thing — closer than you expected in the ways that matter, a little different in the small ones. That is what handmade means, and it is the whole point.
