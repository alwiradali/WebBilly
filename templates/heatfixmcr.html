Why choose a Gas Safe registered engineer?

When it comes to gas, it isn't worth taking a risk. Choosing the wrong engineer doesn't just cost money — it can put people in the house in danger.

Here is what being on the Gas Safe Register actually means for you:

  • It is the law. Anyone working on a gas appliance in the UK must be on the Gas Safe Register. If a business or an individual is not on that list, they are not legally allowed to touch your gas — however good they sound.
  • Far less risk of an accident. Badly done gas work causes fires, explosions and carbon monoxide poisoning. A registered engineer is assessed on the exact appliances they are allowed to work on, and reassessed every five years.
  • Fewer expensive mistakes. The wrong flue, an undersized pipe run or a boiler that was never commissioned properly all cost far more to put right later than they would have cost to do correctly first time.
  • Your warranty stays intact. Almost every manufacturer voids the warranty on a boiler installed or serviced by someone unregistered — and your home insurance may take the same view after a claim.
  • You can check, in a minute. Every registered engineer carries an ID card with a licence number and the categories of work they are qualified for. Ask to see it. Mine is No. 627019, and it comes out on the doorstep before you have to ask.

Questions

The things
everyone asks.

If yours isn't here, ring and ask. You'll get a straight answer whether or not it turns into a job.

Are you Gas Safe registered?

Yes. Every gas job is carried out by a Gas Safe registered engineer, and the ID card comes out on the doorstep before you have to ask. You can check any engineer yourself on the Gas Safe Register website using the licence number on the card.

Do you do landlord gas safety certificates?

Yes — CP12 landlord gas safety records for every gas appliance in the property, issued the same visit. Landlords in England and Wales must have one carried out every twelve months by a Gas Safe registered engineer, and must give the tenant a copy.

How long does a new boiler take to fit?

A straight combi swap in the same spot is usually a single day. Moving the boiler, converting from a back boiler, or going from a tank system to a combi is normally two days. We'll tell you which yours is when we quote, not halfway through.

Why does my boiler keep losing pressure?

Almost always one of three things: a leak somewhere on the pipework or a radiator valve, a failed expansion vessel inside the boiler, or a pressure relief valve passing water outside. Topping it up daily hides the fault rather than fixing it — it's worth getting looked at.

Does my boiler really need servicing every year?

Yes, and two reasons matter more than the rest. A service is where a blocked condensate, a sooting burner or a tired seal gets caught before it strands you in January — and nearly every manufacturer's warranty is void without a service recorded every twelve months. It's also the check that keeps combustion clean, which is what keeps carbon monoxide out of the room.

What areas do you cover?

Manchester and Stockport, and the whole belt between them — Salford, Chorlton, Didsbury, Withington, Burnage, Heaton Moor, Cheadle, Cheadle Hulme, Gatley, Bramhall, Reddish and the rest. Sixty-one areas in all. If you're a bit outside that, ring and ask; the answer is usually yes.

Are you insured?

Yes — HeatFix Mcr Limited carries public liability insurance, on top of being Gas Safe registered. If a landlord, letting agent or managing agent needs to see the certificate before work starts, ask and it gets sent over.
