1930s semis and what is left in them

Burnage is mostly interwar: 1930s semis, some later estate housing, kitchens and garages that have had a boiler in them since long before combis were normal. It is five minutes from us and one of the areas we cover every week.

A house of this age has usually been through two or three boilers, and each one was fitted around whatever was already there. The flue goes where the last flue went, the pipework is a mix of ages, and there is often a hot water cylinder sitting redundant in the airing cupboard. None of that is a problem in itself, but it is worth knowing about before anybody quotes for a swap.

  • Boiler swaps in kitchens and garages
  • Back boilers and old floor-standing units removed
  • Redundant cylinders and tanks taken out
  • Pipework upgraded where the old runs will not do

Servicing, repairs and certificates

The yearly service is the thing that keeps the warranty alive and catches the fault before January does. If you let the property, the gas safety certificate is a legal requirement every twelve months and it covers the cooker and the fire too.

  • Annual boiler servicing
  • Breakdowns, no heating and no hot water
  • Landlord gas safety certificates (CP12)
  • Radiators, valves and power flushing
