How to make cut roses last twice as long

Seven things that genuinely change how long a bouquet lives, and two that do nothing at all.

Most bouquets die of thirst rather than age. A rose drinks a surprising amount in its first two days out of the wrapping, and almost every trick worth knowing is really about keeping that water moving up the stem.

The seven that work

1. Cut the stems again, on a slant, under water

The moment a stem leaves water it starts to seal itself. Cut two centimetres off at an angle before the flowers go in the vase, and do it with the stem under the tap if you can. A slanted cut also stops the end sitting flat on the bottom of the vase, where it cannot drink.

2. Strip every leaf that would sit below the water

Leaves under water rot within a day and turn the vase cloudy. That cloud is bacteria, and bacteria block the stem. This single step is worth more than any sachet.

3. Use the flower food, and use all of it

It is not a gimmick. It is sugar to feed the flower, an acidifier to help the water travel, and a biocide to keep the bacteria down. Half a sachet in a full vase does very little.

4. Change the water every other day

Every other day, not every week. Rinse the vase while you are there. If you do nothing else on this list, do this one.

5. Keep them cool, and out of the sun

A windowsill in July is the worst place in the house. A hallway is usually the best. Cold slows a rose down, which is exactly what you want.

6. Move them away from the fruit bowl

Ripening fruit gives off ethylene, a gas that tells a flower it is time to go over. A bowl of bananas next to a bouquet can take days off it.

7. Take out anything that has gone

One collapsing stem speeds up everything around it. Pull it out, re-cut the rest and the bouquet often gets another three days.

The two that do nothing

Aspirin in the water does not work in a domestic vase; the dose that would help is not the dose anybody uses. Nor does a copper coin, which is a lovely idea from a time when coins were actually copper.

If someone tells you lemonade in the water works, they are half right and half dangerous. The sugar helps. The rest of it feeds bacteria faster than the sugar feeds the rose.

What to expect

Roses treated properly give seven to ten days indoors. Garden roses, which open faster and more beautifully, give five to seven. Hydrangea will drink through its petals as well as its stem, so if a head starts to droop, submerge the whole thing in cool water for twenty minutes and it usually comes back.
